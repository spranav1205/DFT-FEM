**Goal:** Develop a scalable fast-diagonalization (FD)-based overlapping additive Schwarz preconditioner for a matrix-free, conforming finite-element Poisson solver on **nested uniformly refined meshes**: a large coarse cube containing a smaller refined cube, which contains an even more finely refined region. Exploit tensor-product structure within regular regions while handling coarse–fine interfaces and hanging nodes separately, with the goal of reducing CG iterations and total solve time relative to Jacobi on modern CPU/GPU architectures.

**Methods explored:** FD correction on selected DOFs performed no better than Jacobi. Additive matrix splitting with a Neumann-series approximation was abandoned because singular component matrices required regularization that destroyed FD-compatible tensor structure. An intermediate-mesh FD baseline remains untested.

**Current approach:** Overlapping additive Schwarz with partition-of-unity weighting,
\[
M^{-1}=\sum_i R_i^T W_i A_i^{-1}W_iR_i,
\qquad \sum_iR_i^TW_iR_i=I.
\]
Local systems currently use UMFPACK. Hanging-node constraints are applied globally, not within patches; patches are defined by physical-coordinate bounding boxes and assembled from raw cell contributions.

**Validation completed:** Tested slab/octant patches on uniform 3D meshes; corner/edge/center patches on a 2D mesh with one refined region; and 15 corner/face/center patches on a 3D mesh with one refined region. Overlap, coverage, and higher polynomial degrees were checked. The 3D patch-box volume was approximately \(1.29\times\) the domain volume.

**Next steps:**
1. Replace UMFPACK local solves with FD and compare application cost, CG iterations, and total solve time against Jacobi and direct Schwarz.
2. Extend patch generation to nested refinement levels while separating regular tensor-product patches from coarse–fine interface patches.
3. Validate coverage, partition-of-unity weights, and overlap costs across different nesting depths and mesh sizes.
4. Benchmark scalability and CPU/GPU performance to determine when FD's additional cost is justified by fewer CG iterations.
