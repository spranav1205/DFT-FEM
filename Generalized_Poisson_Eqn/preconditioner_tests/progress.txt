**Goal:** Develop a scalable **fast-diagonalization (FD)-based preconditioner for a matrix-free, conforming finite-element Poisson solver on locally refined meshes with hanging nodes**, with emphasis on performance on modern CPU/GPU architectures. The desired construction should exploit exact tensor-product/FD structure wherever the mesh is regular, while handling non-tensor-product refinement interfaces separately, and should ultimately reduce CG iterations and total solve time relative to Jacobi.

**Methods explored and abandoned:** A coarse structured mesh + FD correction applied to the relevant DOFs performed no better than Jacobi, even with Jacobi added on the remaining DOFs. Splitting the matrix into additive pieces and applying a Neumann-series approximation produced singular matrices; regularizing them destroyed the tensor decomposition FD needs, so this was abandoned. An intermediate-mesh-plus-FD baseline is still on the list but not yet tried.

**Main direction: overlapping additive Schwarz with (eventually) FD-solvable local subproblems.** The architecture is

$$
M^{-1}r=\sum_i R_i^T W_i A_i^{-1}W_iR_i,
\qquad
\sum_iR_i^TW_iR_i=I,
$$

with $A_i$ currently a direct (UMFPACK) solve of each patch's own local stiffness matrix, and $W_i$ diagonal, $1/(\text{number of patches whose interior contains that dof})$ — i.e. plain partition-of-unity weighting, not just the two-way $1/2$ case.

**Validation completed this round:**

1. *Uniform 3D mesh, no hanging nodes* (original prototype): 4-patch (slab) and 8-patch (octant) structured splits with 1-cell overlap, compared against Jacobi via CG iteration count and timing. Architecture confirmed sound in the simplest setting.
2. *2D, one locally refined block, hanging nodes present:* 8x8 coarse grid, center 2x2 coarse block refined once (one ring of hanging-node interface around a 4x4 fine block). Patch family generalized to **corner + edge + center**, all specified as physical-coordinate bounding boxes rather than cell-count ranges, which is what lets a single box transparently span a mix of coarse and fine cells.
3. *3D, one locally refined block, hanging nodes present:* 8x8x8 coarse grid, center 2x2x2 coarse block refined once (4x4x4 fine block). Same idea extended to **8 corner (octant) + 6 face + 1 (larger) center** patches — 15 total. Overlap between every adjacent pair of patches (corner–face, corner–center, face–center, and face–face along shared cube edges) was checked algebraically and confirmed nonempty, so the partition-of-unity structure holds throughout, not just at the domain corners.
4. **Higher FE degree tested, method still holds.** The corner/edge/center (2D) and corner/face/center (3D) constructions were validated at higher polynomial degree, not just the original low-order case — the Schwarz architecture continues to work as DOF count per cell grows, which was an open question going in.

**Key implementation decision, now settled: patches never apply hanging-node constraints internally.** Patch boxes are *not* required to have the coarse/fine interface sit exactly on their outer boundary — in practice it usually sits strictly inside corner/face/center patches. Each patch is simply assembled by raw per-cell integration over whichever cells (coarse or fine) fall inside its box, with `AffineConstraints` never invoked locally; a hanging-node dof interior to a patch is treated as an ordinary free local unknown, identical to any other interior dof. Only the outer box boundary (via a geometric "does this dof's support point touch the box edge" test) determines what a patch owns, which is also what makes the overlap weighting well-defined. A per-dof Jacobi fallback exists for the (currently unused, but retained as a safety net) case where a dof ends up interior to zero patches.

**Overlap sizing note:** for the 3D 15-patch construction, edge/face patches deliberately bleed only a *half* coarse-cell (one fine-cell layer) past their 3-coarse-cell core, not a full coarse-cell's worth — this keeps the overlap band thin and deliberate rather than accidentally doubling up with the corner patches, which already claim the full innermost coarse cell at their corner. Total patch-box volume for the 15-patch layout came to about 1.29x the domain volume, i.e. ~29% of the domain is multiply covered — a useful order-of-magnitude reference for tuning overlap cost as the method generalizes.

**Next steps (current focus):**

- **Swap in fast diagonalization for the local solves.** Replace each patch's direct UMFPACK factorization of $A_i$ with an FD solve, without changing the surrounding Schwarz/weighting structure, and measure whether the resulting CG-iteration reduction actually offsets FD's own overhead — i.e. quantify the iterations-saved-vs-cost-per-application tradeoff rather than assuming FD is a win by default.
- **Stress-test with multiple random refinements.** Move past the single hand-placed center block to scenes with several, randomly placed/sized refined regions, to increase problem size and interface complexity and check that the patch construction and overlap logic still behave (no uncovered dofs, no pathological overlap blow-up) under less controlled geometry.
- **Develop a general patch-generation algorithm.** Replace the current hand-specified box lists (corner/edge/center for one block; corner/face/center for one block) with an algorithm that, given an arbitrary set of refined regions, automatically generates a base tiling of structured overlapping patches over the unrefined bulk plus one local corner/face/center-style patch group per refined region — this is the piece needed before the random-refinement stress test above is really meaningful.
- **Test behavior when a cell is skipped from the FD/Schwarz treatment entirely and handled by Jacobi instead.** I.e. deliberately leave some dof(s) uncovered by every patch (rather than the current "shouldn't happen" case) and confirm the Jacobi-fallback path is a reasonable, well-behaved degradation — this is the mechanism that will actually be exercised once patch generation is automatic and can't always guarantee full coverage by construction.
